# Multiuser

The consensus rules when more than two parties work a plan. Today the parties are two, the User and the LLM, and control passes from one to the other until a thread is resolved. Teams makes the parties many people, and User Management makes the LLM none of them: a run is the person who pressed the button. This plan says what the rules become. It was formalized on 2026-10-19 from the owner's notes; its six decisions were taken the same day (the decisions thread, resolved with no reply): the starter or an Owner resolves, no vote, the narrower waiting rule, anyone with write access applies and Answer means answering what waits on the presser, the State by Owners only, one run per plan at a time. Who the parties are is Accounts and Roles; how a person is identified is User Management; what the LLM tasks are is LLM Tasks.


## Today

- The rules (`src/Rules.js`) are already written over any number of participants; the two-party feel comes from there being two. A contested thread waits on every participant except the one who replied last. Only the owner resolves. Only the llm applies. Anyone changes a plan's State at any time.
- Consensus means: someone with authority accepts the outcome stated in the last reply, and the llm applies it as one revision. There is no vote, and this plan keeps it so.


## Resolved

- **A thread is resolved by the one who started it, or by an Owner of the project.** The one who asked knows when it is answered; an Owner can close any thread so that a starter who has gone quiet or left does not hold the plan. The record says who resolved it, as it does today.
- A thread opened by a run is the presser's: the person who pressed Work started the build log thread and resolves it when they have checked the work, as the owner does today. There is no thread without an account as its starter: a person, or a Service account writing through the API.
- Both uses of a thread fit: a back-and-forth with the LLM, resolved by the asker when satisfied, applied with an outcome and no text change; and a change worked out and applied to the plan.
- No majority, no unanimity. A team that wants a second Owner's agreement replies before resolving. A per-project switch, Owners resolve everything, is a later rule if a team asks for it.


## Who a thread waits on

- **Contested:** the starter, the project's Owners, and everyone who has replied in it, minus the one who replied last. Bystanders are not waited on until they join in. (Today: everyone but the last speaker, which is right for two and noise for ten.)
- **Resolved, waiting to be applied:** the starter and the Owners, so that someone who cares sees it through.
- **Applied:** nobody, as today.
- The tally, the counts in the sidebar and the waiting list read from these rules, as they do today.


## Who applies

- **Anyone with write access to the project applies a resolved thread**: an Owner or a Member, in person, by pressing Answer, or through an agent session holding their token; a Service account through the API. Today's rule, only the llm applies, has no one to point at once an LLM is never a user.
- **Answer changes meaning with it.** Today it answers the threads waiting on the LLM. Under these rules it answers the threads waiting on the presser, as the presser: replies on the contested ones, applies the resolved ones, one revision each. The LLM drafts and performs, the person is the author of record. LLM Tasks carries the wording.
- An apply is one revision, by one person, as today; the revision names the person, the token's name and the service's when declared, shown as the byline "<service> by <person>" (User Management).


## The plan's State

- **A plan's State is changed by an Owner only.** Draft to Plan is the Owner's acceptance of the text the threads worked out; Plan to Working is the Owner's word to build; Working to Finished is the Owner's acceptance of the build. Members move the text through their threads; an Owner signs off once, on the document, not on every thread.
- This is the counterweight to the starter resolving: a Member can open a thread, work it with the LLM, resolve and apply it, and the plan's text moves without an Owner; the Owner sees it in the revisions and holds the State. (Today anyone changes a State; on a Local server the owner is everyone, so nothing changes there.)


## LLM tasks in the flow

- **Anyone starts a thread, anyone comments, anyone presses a task, at any time**, within their role: a Watcher reads and presses the read-only tasks only; a Service account writes but presses none (Accounts and Roles). There is no turn order among people; the waiting list says whose attention is wanted, it does not forbid.
- **One run per plan at a time.** A second press on the same plan while a run is on queues behind it and is told so; runs on different plans go together. The revision check at the API already refuses a stale write, so the queue is a convenience, not the safeguard.
- A run's posts are the presser's and wait on the people the rules above say; the run itself is never waited on.


## Out of scope

- Voting, quorums and sign-off by several Owners.
- Notifications beyond the waiting list and the counts (mail, chat) are a later plan.
- Locking a plan while someone edits: the revision check stays the rule.


## Steps

1. The rules in `src/Rules.js`: resolve by starter or Owner, the waiting rules, apply by write access, State by Owners; every rule asserted in `test/Rules.test.js`; the API routes read them.
2. The page: the waiting counts and filters over the new rules; the Resolve button shown to the starter and the Owners; the State menu to Owners.
3. The run queue, one per plan, with its line in the toast and the run record.
4. The guide: the consensus rules page rewritten for a team, and the agent instructions' "Your turn" rewritten as Answer's.
